# Design of SynRM and PMaSynRM Using a Common Industrial Stator Platform

## Citation

S. Suli, Y. Oner, and I. Senol, "Design of SynRM and PMaSynRM using a common industrial stator platform: a new design methodology for transition from industrial applications to electric vehicles," *Engineering Science and Technology, an International Journal*, 2025. DOI: [10.1016/j.estch.2025.102599](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.estch.2025.102599)

## Why This Paper Matters

This paper demonstrates a practical, industrially validated methodology for converting an existing IE2-class induction motor stator platform into SynRM and PMaSynRM configurations using **rotor-only modifications**. The "Efficiency Class Upgrade with Minimum Die Cost" principle eliminates stator/winding tooling costs while enabling IE4/IE5-class performance. The eight-motor comparative study (IM, SynRM, PMaSynRM with ferrite/NdFeB, M700/M400 steels) on a shared 80mm-stack 90-frame stator provides a direct reference for [[common stator platform]] design strategies and rotor barrier optimization parameter ranges.

## Problem Statement

Systematic investigations into the **direct conversion of existing industrial IM platforms into high-efficiency synchronous machines** remain insufficient. Most studies introduce entirely new designs or rely on academic prototypes, neglecting manufacturability, tooling costs, and industrial scalability. The paper addresses this gap by developing SynRM and PMaSynRM configurations compatible with a real production IE2 IM stator.

## Machine / Study Context

- **Reference motor:** 90-frame, 4-pole, 1.5 kW squirrel-cage IM (Gamak AGM2EL 90 L 4b)
- **Stator:** 36-slot, 400V, 50Hz, 80mm stack length
- **IE2 reference efficiency:** 81.5%
- **IE4 comparison motor:** 135mm stack length, M400-50A steel, 86.76% efficiency
- **Lamination steels tested:** M700-50A (standard) and M400-50A (low-loss)
- **Magnet types:** NdFeB and Ferrite (both limited to max 12% of rotor volume)
- **FEA tool:** ANSYS Maxwell 2D
- **Experimental validation:** IE2 and IE4 IM prototypes tested per IEC 60034-1; simulation vs test deviation ~0.3% torque, ~0.3% efficiency
- **Manufacturer:** GAMAK Electric Motors (Istanbul, Turkey) — production-line validated

## Method / Theory

### Design Workflow
1. **Preliminary parametric scan** of rotor geometry variables on fixed stator
2. **MOGA-based multi-objective optimization** of rotor laminations
3. **Magnet integration** into optimized SynRM rotor → PMaSynRM
4. **FEA-based comparison** of all 8 configurations on shared stator
5. **Experimental validation** of IM prototypes to confirm simulation accuracy

### Rotor Geometry Parameters (4-flux-barrier SynRM)

| Parameter | Symbol | Range | Selected Value | Purpose |
|-----------|--------|-------|----------------|---------|
| Rotor position | — | 0–90° | 0° | Best torque and lowest ripple |
| Control angle | β | 0–0.5 rad | 0.174 rad (31.32°) | Optimal efficiency and PF |
| Bridge thickness | W | 0–3 mm | 0.5 mm | Mechanical strength vs performance |
| Barrier width | B0 | 1–4 mm | 4 mm | Positive impact on torque/efficiency |
| Rib thickness | H | 0.5–2.5 mm | 1.0–1.5 mm | Prevents saturation, limits leakage |
| Barrier sharpness | R0 | 0–2 mm | 0 mm | Facilitates magnet placement |
| Barrier tip radius | R1 | 0–1 mm | 0.3 mm | Reduces torque ripple |
| First barrier position | Rb | 15.5–18.5 mm | 17 mm | Torque/ripple balance |
| Inter-barrier distance | Y0 | 1–5.5 mm | 5.2 mm | Overall performance |

## Important Equations

### Objective Function 1: Efficiency × Torque (Maximize)

$$g_1 = \eta \times T \rightarrow \text{max}$$

- **Original notation:** g1: ηxT → Maximized
- **Variables:** η = efficiency [%], T = torque [Nm]
- **Units:** dimensionless product (normalized)
- **Assumptions:** Both η and T are evaluated at rated operating point; higher product indicates better load-condition performance

### Objective Function 2: Efficiency / Torque Ripple (Maximize)

$$g_2 = \frac{\eta}{TR} \rightarrow \text{max}$$

- **Original notation:** g2: η/TR → Maximized
- **Variables:** η = efficiency [%], TR = torque ripple [%]
- **Units:** dimensionless ratio
- **Assumptions:** Higher ratio indicates smoother, quieter operation with good efficiency

### Objective Function 3: Combined Efficiency-Torque / Torque Ripple (Maximize)

$$g_3 = \frac{\eta \times T}{TR} \rightarrow \text{max}$$

- **Original notation:** g3: ηxT/TR → Maximized
- **Variables:** η = efficiency [%], T = torque [Nm], TR = torque ripple [%]
- **Units:** dimensionless composite
- **Assumptions:** Balances torque production, efficiency, and smoothness simultaneously

### Conditional Constraints

$$\eta > 84.98\%, \quad T > 9.55 \text{ Nm}, \quad TR < 10\%$$

- **Original notation:** η > 89.98%, T > 9.55 Nm, TR < %10 (preliminary targets were higher; final thresholds relaxed based on parametric analysis)
- **Note:** The paper states η > 89.98% in one location and η > 84.98% in the conditional constraints section. The value 84.98% appears to be the actual preliminary design result used as threshold.

## Key Design Insights

1. **Common stator platform enables three motor types** (IM → SynRM → PMaSynRM) with zero stator tooling cost — only rotor laminations change.

2. **Barrier width B0 = 4 mm** (maximum allowed) consistently improved torque and efficiency across all configurations.

3. **Bridge thickness W = 0.5 mm** is the practical minimum for mechanical safety; constrained during MOGA optimization.

4. **Rib thickness H = 1.0 mm** (final design) provided similar electromagnetic performance to H = 1.5 mm but with more robust mechanical structure and better tolerance to manufacturing deviations.

5. **7th magnet placement scenario** (magnets in all barrier layers, radially oriented) provided the most balanced performance for both NdFeB and ferrite magnets.

6. **M400 low-loss steel** consistently improved efficiency and power factor across all motor types compared to M700.

7. **SynRM torque ripple < 5%** vs IM at 15–17% — synchronous topologies inherently superior for smoothness.

8. **Simulation vs prototype validation:** Torque/efficiency deviation ~0.3%, power factor slightly lower in test (~5% higher no-load current due to material variations) — within acceptable limits.

9. **NdFeB PMaSynRM with M400** achieves IE5 class (90.25% efficiency, PF > 0.89) — highest performer.

10. **Ferrite PMaSynRM** offers competitive performance (87–89% efficiency) without rare-earth dependency — cost-effective alternative.

## Optimization Setup

- **Method:** Multi-Objective Genetic Algorithm (MOGA)
- **Optimizer:** Coupled with ANSYS Maxwell 2D FEA
- **Design variables:** 9 rotor geometric parameters (W, H, B0, R0, R1, Rb, Y0, rotor position, control angle)
- **Objectives:** 3 composite functions (g1, g2, g3) + conditional constraints
- **Fixed stator:** 36-slot, 80mm stack, identical winding for all motor types
- **Bridge thickness:** Fixed at 0.5 mm for mechanical safety in later optimization runs
- **Pareto-optimal solutions:** 10 designs generated; Design 9 (H = 1.0 mm) selected as final

## Limitations / Caveats

1. **Limited to one frame size:** 90-frame, 4-pole, 1.5 kW — other frames/poles require re-parameterization.
2. **No experimental validation for SynRM/PMaSynRM:** Only IM prototypes were manufactured and tested. SynRM/PMaSynRM results are FEA-based only (Unverified experimentally).
3. **2D FEA only:** 3D effects (end-winding, axial flux variations) not captured.
4. **Steady-state analysis only:** No transient/dynamic or thermal modeling for synchronous configurations.
5. **Magnet volume cap at 12%:** May not represent optimal magnet utilization in all scenarios.
6. **No cost analysis:** Relative cost of NdFeB vs ferrite vs magnet-free configurations not quantified.
7. **NdFeB PMaSynRM torque ripple high (~17%):** Despite high efficiency, ripple may limit NVH-sensitive applications without additional mitigation.
8. **VFD required:** SynRM and PMaSynRM cannot operate from direct grid connection — adds system-level cost not captured in motor-only comparison.
