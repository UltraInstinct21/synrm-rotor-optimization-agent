# Metamodel-based Optimization of Synchronous Reluctance Motor Rotor

## Citation
Orlova, S., Pugachov, V., Auzins, J., & Rassölkin, A. "Metamodel-based Optimization of Synchronous Reluctance Motor Rotor." (Conference paper, likely IEEE, based on references context).

## Why This Paper Matters
Demonstrates a resource-saving rotor optimization technique using metamodels (local polynomial approximations via Legendre polynomials) instead of exhaustive FEA sweeps. Provides a practical workflow: Latin Hypercube DOE → FEA → metamodel synthesis → optimization → experimental validation. The method reduced 31 FEA runs to a polynomial surrogate, achieving optimal rotor geometry for a 1.1 kW SynRM with measured 89% peak efficiency.

## Problem Statement
SynRM rotor geometry (flux barriers, ribs) must be optimized for maximum electromagnetic torque while satisfying mechanical and manufacturing constraints. Full FEA-based optimization is computationally expensive; a metamodel-based approach is needed to find the optimal design with minimal FEA calls.

## Machine / Study Context
- **Base machine:** Modified 1.1 kW W21 WEG induction motor stator (IE2 class)
- **Rotor type:** TLA (transversally laminated anisotropy) with rectangular flux barriers
- **Stator:** 36 slots, outer diameter 139 mm, inner diameter 91.2 mm, stack length 110 mm, 47 turns, 2 parallel paths, coil pitch 1-8, filling factor 69.48%, wire diameter 0.56 mm
- **Control:** Open-loop vector control via YASKAWA GA 500 VFD
- **Rated conditions:** 380 V, 50 Hz, 2.69 A, cos φ = 0.76, η = 81.6% (original induction motor)

## Method / Theory
Response Surface Methodology (RSM) with local polynomial approximations (Legendre polynomials, 14 terms selected via cross-validation). Latin Hypercube DOE with 31 combinations generated by EDAOpt software. Each DOE point evaluated via 2D FEA. Metamodel fitted to electromagnetic torque response, then optimized analytically.

## Important Equations (with normalized form, original notation, variables, units, assumptions)

### Eq. 1 — Response Surface General Form
$$y = f(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_q) + \varepsilon$$
- **y**: response (dependent variable), e.g. electromagnetic torque
- **x₁, x₂, …, xq**: independent variables (design parameters)
- **ε**: experimental error term
- **Units:** y in Nm (for torque); x in respective units per Table II
- **Assumptions:** independent variables are continuous and controllable with negligible error; response is random

### Eq. 2 — SynRM Electromagnetic Torque
$$T_{em} = \frac{m \cdot p \cdot U^2}{2 \cdot \omega} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{x_q} - \frac{1}{x_d}\right) \cdot \sin 2\gamma$$
- **Tem**: electromagnetic torque [Nm]
- **p**: number of pole pairs
- **m**: number of phases
- **U**: phase voltage [V]
- **ω**: angular frequency of armature current [rad/s]
- **xd**: direct axis synchronous reactance [Ω]
- **xq**: quadrature axis synchronous reactance [Ω]
- **γ**: load angle between supply voltage and fundamental harmonic of EMF [rad or °]
- **Assumptions:** steady-state operation, sinusoidal MMF, no saturation cross-coupling
- **Normalized form:** $T_{em} \propto \left(\frac{1}{x_q} - \frac{1}{x_d}\right) \sin 2\gamma$ — torque is proportional to saliency difference

### Eq. 3 — Insulation Ratio (Flux Barrier Coefficient)
$$K_W = \frac{w_b}{w_g}$$
- **Kw**: insulation ratio (dimensionless)
- **wb**: sum of flux air barrier widths [mm]
- **wg**: sum of flux guide widths [mm]
- **Range:** Kw = 0 → solid iron rotor (no saliency); Kw = 1 → equal barrier and guide widths
- **Assumptions:** optimal inductance reported at Kw ≈ 1 (50:50 split) per [8], [11], [12]

### Eq. 4 — Torque Ripple
$$T_{ripple} = \frac{T_{max} - T_{min}}{T_{avg}} \cdot 100$$
- **Tripple**: torque ripple [%]
- **Tmax**: maximum torque in investigated time interval [Nm]
- **Tmin**: minimum torque in investigated time interval [Nm]
- **Tavg**: average torque [Nm]
- **Assumptions:** evaluated over one electrical cycle at steady state

## Key Design Insights
1. **Saliency ratio is paramount:** Torque is directly proportional to (1/xq − 1/xd); maximizing the d/q inductance difference is the primary design objective.
2. **Insulation ratio and barrier count dominate torque:** Cross-validation metamodel shows electromagnetic torque peak difference depends mainly on Kw (x4) and barrier number (x5).
3. **Diminishing returns on barriers:** >5 barriers provide negligible torque improvement and worsen mechanical integrity.
4. **Rib thickness trade-off:** Thin tangential ribs maximize torque but are hard to manufacture; thick ribs reduce torque. Minimum viable ribs used (1 mm radial, 1 mm tangential in optimum).
5. **Optimal current angle ≠ 45°:** Maximum torque shifts to larger current angles under load due to increased stator magnetizing force.
6. **Rotor skewing:** 1 stator slot pitch skew (4°) reduces torque ripple from 53% to 7% but costs ~20% average torque.

### Method
- Latin Hypercube DOE, 31 points, optimized via Mean Square Error criterion (EDAOpt software)
- FEA for each DOE point
- Metamodel: Legendre polynomial, 14 terms (selected by cross-validation, value = 24.13)
- Objective: maximize electromagnetic torque

## Results (numerical)
- **Optimal xd:** 153 Ω; **optimal xq:** 29.1 Ω → saliency ratio ≈ 5.26
- **Peak efficiency:** 89% at 7.5 Nm, 1500 rpm
- **Torque ripple without skew:** 53%
- **Torque ripple with 1-slot-pitch skew (4°):** 7% (but ~20% torque reduction)
- **No-load test:** Phase current ~1.33 A across voltage range (220–385 V)
- **Load test at rated speed (1500 rpm):**
  - 2.5 Nm → 87% efficiency, 1.6 A, 410 W input
  - 7.5 Nm → 89% efficiency, 2.7 A, 1280 W input (peak η)
  - 11.5 Nm → 72% efficiency, 5.01 A, 2460 W input

## Limitations / Caveats
- 2D FEA only — does not capture end-winding effects, axial flux variations, or 3D skew effects accurately
- Open-loop vector control limits experimental characterization; closed-loop would give different operating point data
- Metamodel is local polynomial — validity limited to the design space explored (extrapolation risky)
- Mechanical constraints handled informally (not as explicit constraints in the optimization)
- Skewing benefit analyzed only at one slot pitch; no multi-slip optimization
